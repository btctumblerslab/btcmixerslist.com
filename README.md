What to look for in a [crypto mixer](https://btcmixerslist.com/)

Custody comes first. A CoinJoin wallet never lets go of your keys, while a hosted service holds your coins until the delay ends. Next is the crowd: the more identical outputs in a round, the harder yours is to single out. Third is the full price, meaning the service fee plus your share of the mining fee.

Then come the details that separate solid tools from risky ones: Tor support out of the box, no account or email, a delay you control, and an operator with a public track record. A service with no history, no clear fee schedule and pressure to deposit quickly is the one to skip.

How a [BTC mixer](https://btcmixerslist.com/) works

- Coins go in
  
You register inputs with a CoinJoin coordinator, or send coins to a hosted service’s deposit address.

- The round
  
Your inputs join many others in one transaction, or wait in a shared pool while the delay runs.

- Fresh coins out
  
Equal outputs land at new addresses, with no simple on-chain link back to the input.
